# What soil sounds tell us

A contact microphone can register vibrations from invertebrates moving, feeding or communicating. Weather, machinery, footsteps and the properties of the soil also affect the signal. The Soil Acoustic Meter (SAM) sends recordings to a portal that calculates the proprietary Soil Acoustic Quality Index (SAQI). An acoustic index summarises features of a recording. Connecting it to the number of animals, the variety of species or crop performance requires a separate comparison for each outcome. [SRC-002](sources.md#src-002), [SRC-004](sources.md#src-004), [SRC-023](sources.md#src-023)

## Findings from field and controlled studies

| Study | Findings and implications for a field user |
| --- | --- |
| South Australian woodland restoration, 2024 | Researchers collected 240 recordings across six plots over five days and counted invertebrates. Revegetated and remnant woodland had more complex and diverse soundscapes than cleared plots. This shows an ecological signal in one landscape using research indices. Its transfer to SAQI on UK farms requires a separate test. [SRC-019](sources.md#src-019) |
| Constructed soundscapes from three countries, 2024 | Metcalf and colleagues tested 14 indices on one-minute soundscapes with known numbers and types of sound events. Combinations predicted the number of events well. They were less successful at measuring the variety of sounds across recordings or how evenly different sounds occurred. Many clicks can come from one active animal. A coauthor was affiliated with Baker Consultants. [SRC-020](sources.md#src-020) |
| Oxfordshire pasture, research briefing, 2026 | Researchers recorded for 24 hours at each of ten grassland sites and collected invertebrates. Several indices correlated with the number of earthworms collected. The team identifies moisture, soil type and management history as possible explanations for differences among sites. The sampling duration also differs from SAM’s three minutes. [SRC-021](sources.md#src-021) |
| Soil moisture experiment, 2026 | A controlled experiment with geophones detected earthworm movement more often in wetter soil. More detections after rain could reflect a change in activity or in how easily movements are detected, even with worms present on both visits. Record moisture and compare similar conditions. [SRC-022](sources.md#src-022) |

## Validation of the commercial score

The public sources reviewed provide no independent, peer-reviewed estimate of how accurately commercial SAQI predicts invertebrate counts or species richness on previously unseen farms. They also leave open how large a change exceeds normal repeat-to-repeat variation. A separate Warwick-led review identifies uncertainty over the origin of recorded signals, the way vibrations travel through soil, and the representativeness of datasets. AHDB’s broader assessment also measures structure, pH, extractable nutrients, earthworms and organic matter. [SRC-023](sources.md#src-023), [SRC-024](sources.md#src-024), [SRC-009](sources.md#src-009)

## How to investigate a change in score

Revisit marked points in the same season and record soil moisture, recent rain, temperature and field operations. For a management trial, include a comparable untreated area and obtain paired earthworm counts at a subset of points. Inspect the spread of scores within each batch alongside its mean. If the difference disappears on repeat visits, there is little basis for changing management on the strength of the first reading. [SRC-003](sources.md#src-003), [SRC-021](sources.md#src-021), [SRC-024](sources.md#src-024)
